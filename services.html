Our Services

Mezzanine Loans

A mezzanine loan is a hybrid of debt and equity financing that sits between senior debt (the primary mortgage) and the borrower's equity. It is typically secured by the borrower’s ownership interest in the property rather than the real estate itself. In the event of a default, the lender can quickly convert the debt into an equity position, making it a higher-risk, higher-yield option often used to fill funding gaps in large projects.

Hard Money Loans

Hard money loans are short-term, asset-backed loans issued by private individuals or companies rather than traditional banks. Because underwriting is heavily focused on the value of the collateral (the property) rather than the borrower’s creditworthiness, these loans can be funded incredibly quickly. However, they come with significantly higher interest rates and fees, making them ideal for quick acquisitions or fix-and-flip projects.

Bridge Loans

A bridge loan is a short-term financing solution designed to "bridge" the gap between an immediate funding need and a long-term debt solution or property sale. Typically lasting from six months to three years, bridge loans are frequently used to acquire a property quickly, execute light renovations, or stabilize an asset's occupancy before applying for permanent financing.

Construction Loans

Construction loans are short-term, specialized loans used to fund the ground-up development or major renovation of a property. Unlike traditional loans that pay out in a lump sum, construction loans are disbursed in stages—known as "draws"—as specific building milestones are met. These loans generally require a detailed budget, architectural plans, and typically feature interest-only payments during the construction phase.

CMBS Loans

Commercial Mortgage-Backed Securities (CMBS) loans, also known as conduit loans, are fixed-rate commercial real estate loans that are pooled together and sold to investors as bonds on the secondary market. Because they are securitized, CMBS loans offer highly competitive, fixed interest rates and non-recourse terms (shielding personal assets). However, they feature incredibly strict underwriting guidelines and rigid prepayment penalties.
